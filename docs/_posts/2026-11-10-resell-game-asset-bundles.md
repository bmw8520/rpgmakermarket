# How to Resell Game Asset Bundles the Right Way

![Hand-drawn game asset bundle title card](https://media.babylovegrowth.ai/blog-images/organization-61610/1794166725082_Hand-drawn-game-asset-bundle-title-card.jpeg)

You can resell a game asset bundle only when the license says you can. Most marketplace licenses let you ship the assets inside a game but not hand out the raw files. So the safe routes are bundles of your own work, bundles from creators who grant redistribution, and whitelabel-ready packs that are sold for resale on purpose. This guide shows you how to tell them apart, how to build a bundle buyers want, and how to list it.

## TL;DR

- Reselling means passing on the asset files themselves. Shipping assets inside your finished game is a different permission.
- Marketplace licenses often allow commercial use in a compiled game but [typically do not allow redistribution of raw files](https://blog.kongregate.com/hc/en-us/articles/44269612548877-ASSETS-What-license-types-apply-to-game-assets). Read the exact license every time.
- Safe sources for resale bundles: your own creations, assets with written redistribution rights, and packs sold as whitelabel or resale-ready.
- Build a bundle around one engine and one job, such as RPG Maker MZ town tilesets or loop-ready battle music.
- Keep a record for every file: license name, a saved copy of the terms, and what the license allows.

## What "reselling" a game asset bundle actually means

Two very different things hide behind this search.

**Selling a bundle of assets you made.** You own the work, so you decide the terms. You gather your sprites, tilesets, sound effects or plugins into one package, price it below the total of the parts, and sell it.

**Reselling assets someone else made.** Now you are redistributing another creator's files. That needs permission. As one [asset-licensing explainer for indie developers](https://drillimation.com/2025/02/07/an-introduction-for-indie-developers-using-preexisting-assets-what-developers-should-know-about-asset-licenses/) puts it, reusing an asset can infringe the creator's exclusive rights unless the use is authorized or falls under a copyright exception such as fair use.

There is also a third case people mix in: using a bought asset inside your own game. That is the normal use of a marketplace license, and it does not give you the right to pack the same files into a bundle for others.

Keep this split in mind and most of the confusion goes away. Using an asset in a game is one permission. Giving the asset to another developer is another.

## Check the license before you bundle anything

A license is per file, not per author. The [Free Game Dev Assets license guide](https://tmhsdigital.github.io/Free-Game-Dev-Assets/guide/licenses/) notes that one creator's uploads can carry different terms, and that marketplace assets labeled "free" are often commercial in-engine use only, with no redistribution.

That is why a bundle built from a dozen packs can break in one place. One file with a no-redistribution clause is enough.

### The terms to look for

Before you redistribute, modify, bundle or sell, check these points. They follow the checklists from [Kongregate's asset license overview](https://blog.kongregate.com/hc/en-us/articles/44269612548877-ASSETS-What-license-types-apply-to-game-assets) and [3DSkillUp's guide to asset licenses](https://3dskillup.art/3d-asset-licenses-for-games/).

| Question | Why it matters for resale |
|---|---|
| Is commercial use allowed? | Selling anything built on the asset is commercial. |
| Is redistribution of source or raw files allowed? | Bundling means handing over files, not just a finished game. |
| May you modify the asset? | Reskinning or recoloring before you resell may be banned. |
| Is attribution required? | You must pass the credit requirement on to your buyers. |
| Are there special conditions? | Editorial use, AI restrictions, platform limits or separate creator terms can all apply. |

### "Royalty-free" does not mean free to resell

The phrase describes a fee model. According to [a game asset licensing article on legalbit.top](https://legalbit.top/en/gamedev-game-asset-open-source-licensing/), it does not mean public domain or unrestricted use. The same article lists useful questions to ask of any asset: may you modify it, combine it with other work, distribute it on each platform you plan to use, and let players extract it.

### Price tiers and restrictions

The Drillimation article also observes that the lowest-priced assets tend to carry the most restrictions and the highest-priced assets the fewest. Common limits include which storefronts you can use, how the asset can be used, caps on sales or downloads of your game, and an expiry date on the agreement. Cheap assets are the riskiest to put in a resale bundle.

### Store-specific rules

Platform terms can be stricter than the creator's own. The legalbit article says Unity's Asset Store EULA lets a studio embed a non-restricted asset in a game only together with substantial original content, so the asset is not a substantial part of the product. It also says players may not sell, transfer or lend the asset, and that using assets to train AI models is banned. Read the current terms of any store you source from or list on.

### Creative Commons is not one license

Creative Commons assets look easy to bundle, but the variant decides everything.

- **CC BY-SA:** usable commercially, but share-alike applies to derivatives, according to the [license guide](https://tmhsdigital.github.io/Free-Game-Dev-Assets/guide/licenses/). The same guide says to ship share-alike files in extractable bundles rather than encrypting them into the binary.
- **CC BY-ND:** the same guide calls these generally unusable in games, because interactive sync and remix count as derivatives.
- **NonCommercial variants:** a paid bundle is commercial by definition. Treat these as off limits for resale.

If you want to see how creators label their work, itch.io has browsable lists by license, for example assets under CC BY-SA 4.0, CC BY-NC 4.0 and CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Use them to understand the labels. Always open the individual page and read the terms before you reuse a file.

## Three safe ways to build a resale bundle

### 1. Bundle your own work

This is the cleanest path. You hold the rights, so you set the terms. Tilesets, character sprites, UI icons, sound packs and plugins you made yourself can all go in one package.

### 2. Use assets with written redistribution rights

Some creators explicitly permit redistribution or bundling. Get that in writing, save it with the file, and note the version of the license. A provenance checklist on [GitHub](https://github.com/gamedev-skills/awesome-gamedev-agent-skills/blob/main/skills/disciplines/create-game-assets/references/provenance.md) recommends recording the license name and version, a saved copy or link to the terms, and whether commercial use, modification, redistribution or AI training is restricted. It also points out that shipping a game and publishing editable source files can have different permissions.

### 3. Start from resale-ready packs

Whitelabel-ready resources are made for this. The license is written for commercial use or resale, so you are not guessing. We build our packs this way for RPG Maker MV and MZ developers, which we cover below.

## How to assemble and list a bundle

A bundle is a collection of premade assets that lets a developer start a project fast. [Rokoko's guide to selling game assets](https://www.rokoko.com/insights/sell-game-assets) describes kits and bundles as premade collections that can make a basic game easily, often with models, textures and sound effects, and says selling assets is about being in the right place, in front of the right people, at the right time.

Here is a workflow that holds up.

1. **Pick one engine and one job.** "RPG Maker MZ forest and cave tilesets" is easier to buy than "500 mixed game assets." A buyer should know in five seconds whether the pack fits.
2. **Audit every file.** Make a spreadsheet: file name, creator, license, redistribution allowed (yes or no), attribution text. Drop anything without a clear yes.
3. **Make the files consistent.** Match grid sizes, color palettes and naming. For RPG Maker, that means following the engine's tile and sprite sheet layouts so assets drop in without editing.
4. **Add a license file.** Put a plain text license in the download. State what buyers can do: use in commercial games, modify, and whether they may resell. Say so clearly.
5. **Show it working.** Screenshots in the actual editor, a short GIF, and a list of every file type beat any long description.
6. **Write the listing like a spec sheet.** Engine versions, file formats, count of assets, and the license in one line.
7. **Price it as a bundle.** Offer a visible saving against buying the parts separately, and keep a single clear price.
8. **Update it.** Fix bugs, add files, and tell past buyers. Ongoing updates are a reason to pick your pack over a one-time dump.

The bundle listing mechanics differ by store, and fees and rules change, so check the current seller terms on the platform you pick. On itch.io, for example, the platform has run a bundle-focused sales week that shows how creators package and promote bundles.

## Pricing and demand

There is no formula, but these checks keep you grounded.

- **Search the category.** Look at what is already selling for your engine. If a dozen similar tileset packs exist, find a gap, such as a theme, a genre or a specific era.
- **Compare per-asset value.** If your bundle contains 200 files, a buyer will do the math. Make sure the total looks like a saving.
- **Price to the license.** A pack that allows commercial use and resale is worth more than one that only allows personal projects.
- **Test a small first release.** Launch a focused pack, read the questions buyers ask, then expand.

Be cautious about income stories. Posts about creators making big money from assets are one outcome from one seller. Treat them as inspiration, not a forecast. Sales depend on quality, how unique the pack is, and whether anyone is looking for it.

## Why RPG Maker is a good niche for bundles

![Why RPG Maker is a good niche for bundles](https://media.babylovegrowth.ai/blog-images/organization-61610/1794166783183_Why-RPG-Maker-is-a-good-niche-for-bundles.jpeg)

## A quick pre-launch checklist

- Every file has a license record and a saved copy of the terms.
- No file carries a NonCommercial or NoDerivatives restriction.
- Attribution text is collected in one credits file.
- The bundle license says exactly what buyers may and may not do.
- Screenshots come from the real engine editor.
- The listing names the engine versions, file formats and file counts.
- You know how you will handle updates and buyer questions.

## FAQ

### Can I resell assets I bought from a marketplace?

Usually not as raw files. Commercial marketplace licenses often allow use in a compiled game but [typically do not permit redistribution of raw files](https://blog.kongregate.com/hc/en-us/articles/44269612548877-ASSETS-What-license-types-apply-to-game-assets). Read the specific license, and if redistribution is not clearly allowed, ask the creator for written permission.

### Can I sell a bundle that mixes my art with other creators' art?

Yes, if each outside file's license allows redistribution and you honor its conditions, such as attribution. Your own files are yours to bundle. Keep a record for every outside file so you can show where it came from.

### Is a Creative Commons asset safe to resell?

It depends on the variant. Share-alike licenses carry conditions on derivatives, and NonCommercial licenses rule out a paid bundle. The [license guide](https://tmhsdigital.github.io/Free-Game-Dev-Assets/guide/licenses/) also says NoDerivatives licenses are generally unusable in games. Read the exact terms on each page.

### What should a bundle license say?

It should state whether buyers can use the assets in commercial games, modify them, and resell or redistribute them. It should also carry any attribution requirement from the original creators. Short and specific wins.

### Where should I start if I make RPG Maker content?

Pick one theme, build a small consistent pack, and test it with real projects. You can also look at [our RPG Maker library](https://rpg-maker-plugins.itch.io/) for MZ and MV resources made for commercial use and resale.
